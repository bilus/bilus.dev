Everybody talks about how great AI agents are at producing tons of code. Some
are excited about it. Others (less enthused) talk about switching to bee
farming.

Writing code has never been the difficult part. At least not the kind of code
that usually sort of works and does almost exactly not entirely what it was
supposed to do. Been there, done that, count me in. But solving the right
problems in the right way has always been a tiny bit harder.

But agents can help with that. They can do research for you, document that
third-party integration with the sucky docs. They can do spikes, UX prototypes.
They can even reverse-engineer competitors' products (hush).

For more years than I'm willing to admit, my golden rule for managing work in
projects has been to remove the biggest risks first. So, for instance, you don't
start with logins, signups, and fancy animations when you're building a live
collaboration platform and you've never built a live collaboration platform
before (guess how I know). You start with the live collaboration part. You
prototype, you experiment, you do spikes. And this is where agents can help.

> By the way, you could write a book about risk-driven project management. And
> actually, someone did. One of the most eye-opening books is George Fairbanks'
> [Just Enough Software Architecture: A Risk-Driven Approach](https://books.google.com/books/about/Just_Enough_Software_Architecture.html?id=ITsWdAAzVYMC).
> (No, it's not an affiliate link.)

Need to do a spike or solve an integration puzzle? Sic AI on it. It'll
cheerfully toil away while you're sipping your coffee. You can do one, two,
fifteen spikes in hours or days. Who knows, if you try different approaches, you
may find out what actually holds up with fifty people collaborating on
one document. You can build a whole working prototype. Or three.

Then, hear me out, you throw that experimental code AWAY. Or at least keep it
away from the production code.

Now build the real thing iteratively. Yeah, you use AI, who's crazy enough not to?
But you and your team build it, owning the code, understanding the architecture,
refactoring, arguing with AI and one another. Blood, sweat, and tears.

Slower than vibe coding, faster than vibe coding plus the detours and rewrites
that follow it, way faster than typing every character yourself, and more
enjoyable than reviewing mounds of slop.

Nobody is guessing anymore. The team knows the thing can be done, and the agents
read the spikes instead of solving every problem from scratch.

There's one cardinal rule though. Never, ever show that prototype to your
pointy-haired bosses. They'll ship it.
